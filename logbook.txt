Terrain configurations:

---

February 2026

Baseline robustness experiments.

Tasks completed:

* Evaluated baseline policy across multiple slope angles.
* Conducted repeated trials for each terrain condition.
* Recorded performance metrics automatically.

Experiments:

* Flat terrain
* ±1 degree slopes
* ±3 degree slopes
* ±5 degree slopes
* Later expanded to ±7 degree and ±10 degree slopes

Major observation:
Robot frequently drifted sideways or rotated away from the commanded direction before falling.

Key finding:
Robustness degradation occurs gradually through directional instability rather than immediate collapse.

Actions:

* Added heading-failure detection.
* Added corridor-escape detection.
* Generated trajectory visualisations.

---

March 2026

Failure mode analysis.

Tasks completed:

* Analysed trajectory data.
* Investigated heading drift behaviour.
* Examined relationship between slope angle and failure mode.

Findings:

* Shallow slopes often caused corridor escape.
* Moderate slopes produced large heading errors.
* Steeper slopes caused catastrophic falls.
* Different failure modes emerged at different inclinations.

Contribution identified:
Development of a robustness evaluation framework capable of distinguishing directional failure from catastrophic instability.

======================================================================
EASTER TERM
===========

April 2026

Reward-function investigation.

Tasks completed:

* Analysed original Open Duck reward formulation.
* Reviewed Disney BD-X locomotion reward structure.
* Investigated locomotion stabilisation techniques.

Hypothesis:
Additional slip regularisation may improve terrain robustness by encouraging more stable foot-ground interactions.

Implementation:

* Added explicit slip penalty term to reward function.
* Modified training configuration.
* Prepared retraining experiments.

---

May 2026

Slip-regularised policy training and evaluation.

Tasks completed:

* Retrained policy using modified reward formulation.
* Evaluated policy using identical benchmark framework.
* Compared against baseline controller.

Metrics collected:

* Success rate
* Fall rate
* Episode duration
* Forward progress
* Heading error
* Lateral drift
* Corridor escape

Results:

* Improved behaviour on some shallow slopes.
* Improved stability on selected uphill and downhill conditions.
* Did not eliminate failures on steeper terrain.
* Introduced new behavioural trade-offs.

Key insight:
Reward shaping changed the locomotion strategy rather than uniformly improving robustness.
